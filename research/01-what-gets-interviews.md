# What gets candidates interviews (2025–2026)

Researched 2026-10-21. **[ATS data]** = a vendor's own platform data: real behaviour, but from an interested party whose customers skew toward tech and mid-size or large firms. **[Survey]** = self-reported. **[Vendor blog]** and **[Anecdote]** = weak.

## 1. Interview rates by channel

| Channel | Application → interview | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound (cold apply) | 3% (2021–24 avg) | [Ashby referrals report, 2025](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals) [ATS data, 38M apps] |
| Recruiter-sourced | 25% | same |
| Referral | 40% | same |
| Agency / internal | 42% / 42% | same |

- In the same Ashby data, inbound application→offer fell from 0.7% to 0.2% between 2021 and 2024, and referral application→offer fell from 12.1% to 7.3%. Referrals were only 1% of applications.
- Referred candidates pass the first screen 52% of the time, against 35% overall. A same-function referral reaches interview 37% of the time, against 26% for a referral from outside the function. ([Ashby, May 2026](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/recruiting-operations-benchmarks-talent-trends)) [ATS data]
- Gem: 8% of applicants pass the initial screen and about 0.5% get offers. Referrals convert at 11× the inbound rate. ([Gem 2026 Benchmarks, Dec 2025](https://www.gem.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-recruiting-benchmarks-report); 165M apps) [ATS data]
- Greenhouse, 2025: external job boards brought 49.9% of applications but 22.6% of hires. Company career sites brought 23.8% of applications and 34.4% of hires. Recruiter-sourced candidates were 2.9% of applications and 9.7% of hires, and referrals were 11.6% of hires. ([Greenhouse "The Hire Standard", Mar 2026](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/69c6a67c710b821d52b0d5d3_Greenhouse_Benchmark_Report_March2026_NorthAmerica.pdf)) [ATS data, 640M apps]
- LinkedIn: US applicants with a first-degree connection already working at the company were "nearly 7x more likely to get hired". LinkedIn notes that having the connection does not mean the applicant used it or was referred. Data: 5M members, Jul 2019–Dec 2025. ([LinkedIn release, Sep 3 2026](https://www.webwire.com/ViewPressRel.asp?aId=359951)) [Platform data]
- At one large US firm, referred applicants were 6% of applicants but 29% of hires, and were more than twice as likely as job-board applicants to be interviewed. ([NY Fed SR568, 2013](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr568.pdf)) [Academic. Old, but it matches the current ATS data.]
- **Direct outreach from a candidate to a hiring manager:** I found no primary data on its interview rate. See §5.

## 2. Volume vs tailoring

**Volume trends**
- Ashby: applications per hire have tripled since 2021 to more than 300. Candidates are about 50% less likely to get an interview than five years ago. ([Ashby press release, May 7 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-data-from-ashby-reveals-surge-in-applications-rising-selectivity-and-shifting-recruiter-workloads-302765846.html)) [ATS data]
- Greenhouse: applications per job rose from about 115 (2022) to 244 (2025). Applications per hire were 203 in 2025, up 158%. Recruiters per organization fell 56%. ([Greenhouse, Mar 2026](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/69c6a67c710b821d52b0d5d3_Greenhouse_Benchmark_Report_March2026_NorthAmerica.pdf)) [ATS data]
- 22% of US job seekers use bots or agents to auto-apply, and 34% of recruiters spend up to half their week filtering spam applications. ([Greenhouse, Jul 2025](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2025-workforce-hiring-report); [Nov 2025](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)) [Survey]
- Recruiters respond by cherry-picking "a few great applicants and ignoring the rest" (practitioner quote, Greenhouse Mar 2026). Greenhouse's "My Dream Job" lets each candidate flag one role a month, and those applicants are hired at about 5× the rate of others ([CEO in Fortune, Jul 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/07/27/greenhouse-ceo-daniel-chait-ai-doom-loop-job-seekers-spam-interview-applications-unemployment/)). [Vendor claim, method not published.] Scarce, credible signals of intent beat volume.

**Does tailoring help? Yes, but AI is eroding its value as a signal.**
- On Freelancer.com, employers paid a premium for applications tailored to the job before LLMs. After LLMs that premium disappeared. In the authors' model, top-quintile workers are hired 19% less often. ([Galdin & Silbert, arXiv 2511.08785, Nov 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08785)) [Academic]
- An AI cover-letter tool on a large platform made letters better aligned with job posts and **raised callbacks**. However, the correlation between alignment and callbacks fell 51%, and employers shifted to weighting work history. More time spent editing the AI draft predicted more hires. ([Cui, Dias & Ye, arXiv 2509.25054, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25054)) [Academic]
- In a field experiment with about 480K jobseekers, non-generative help with resume writing (grammar and clarity) raised hires by 8% and employers were no less satisfied. ([Wiles, Munyikwa & Horton, Management Science 2025 / NBER w30886](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30886)) [Academic]
- One jobseeker sent 5,000 LazyApply applications and got about 20 interviews (0.4%). He got the same number from about 300 manual applications. ([Wired via Futurism, Nov 2023](https://futurism.com/the-byte/unemployed-man-ai-apply-5000-jobs-gets-20-interviews)) [Anecdote]
- *Confidence:* no 2025–26 study isolates the effect of tailoring on interview rates. Tailoring still helps somewhat, but polished text no longer sets a candidate apart. Real fit, work history and specific evidence matter more.
- **Applying early:** the claim that it helps (for example "8× more interviews within 96 hours") traces to TalentWorks (2017) and other job-tool vendors. I found no ATS or academic source for it. [Vendor blog. Low confidence.]

## 3. Recruiter attitudes to AI-written materials, detection, and ATS practices

- Hiring-manager surveys disagree. 19.6% would reject an AI-generated resume or cover letter ([TopResume, May 2025](https://topresume.com/career-advice/ai-in-hiring-survey)). 62% say an AI resume *without customization* is more likely to be rejected ([Resume Now, Jun 2025](https://www.resume-now.com/job-resources/careers/ai-applicant-report)). 49% dismiss resumes they suspect are AI-written ([Resume.io, Jan 2025](https://resume.io/blog/resume-rejections)). All three are [Vendor survey]. Their common theme is that recruiters penalize *generic* text, not AI use as such.
- 41% of US job seekers admit using prompt injection, and 22% of hiring managers have found it in resumes. 65% of hiring managers have caught deceptive AI use. ([Greenhouse, Nov 2025](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)) [Survey] Indeed bans "AI prompts or scripts" in resumes ([Indeed guidelines](https://www.indeed.com/help/job-seekers/articles/360028540531-indeed-job-seeker-guidelines?hl=en&co=US)). [Official]
- Gartner predicts that 1 in 4 candidate profiles will be fake by 2028 ([HR Dive, Aug 2025](https://www.hrdive.com/news/fake-job-candidates-ai/757126/)), so expect more identity and fraud checks. [Analyst]
- **Disclosure:** across 13 experiments, including a job-application scenario, disclosing AI use reduced trust. Being *exposed* by a third party for undisclosed AI use reduced it further. ([Schilke & Reimann, OBHDP 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2025.104405)) [Academic]
- **LLM screeners prefer LLM text:** models chose their own resume summaries over human-written ones 67–82% of the time. In simulated pipelines, candidates were 23–60% more likely to be shortlisted. ([Xu et al., arXiv 2509.00462, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00462)) [Academic preprint, simulated pipelines]
- **ATS practices:**
  - All 25 recruiters interviewed use knockout questions (work authorization, licensing, location). Only 2 had content-based auto-reject switched on. ([Enhancv, 2025](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/)) [Vendor, small sample]
  - Greenhouse Real Talent flags bot and fraud applications using phone, email, IP and location signals ([Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/introducing-greenhouse-real-talent)).
  - The "75% rejected by ATS" claim has no published dataset behind it.
- Ashby companies archive candidates who are never interviewed after a median of about 6 days (Ashby, May 2026). [ATS data]

## 4. Auto-apply tools

- **Platform policies** [Official]:
  - Indeed: "Don't use third-party bots or other automated tools to apply for jobs." Indeed may cap daily applications and close accounts ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/help/job-seekers/articles/360028540531-indeed-job-seeker-guidelines?hl=en&co=US)).
  - LinkedIn bans software that will "automate activity", and offending accounts risk restriction ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a1341387)).
  - Greenhouse Real Talent flags "mass applications".
- **Measured outcomes:** I found no independent study of LazyApply, AIApply, Sonara or JobCopilot. The only data point is the Wired anecdote, a 0.4% interview rate.
- **Complaints** [Vendor blogs; unverified]: LazyApply is rated about 2.1–2.3 on Trustpilot, with complaints about broken filters and refunds. The original Sonara shut down in Feb 2024 and locked users out mid-search. AIApply's terms make Auto-Apply non-refundable. JobCopilot users report being sent to scam listings. ([Dreamwork](https://www.dreamworkhq.com/blog/sonara-ai-review), [Wobo](https://www.wobo.ai/blog/lazyapply-review/))

## 5. Cold outreach

- **Candidate → recruiter or hiring manager:** I found **no primary or academic reply-rate data**. Vendor blogs claim 5–34%, and the more careful ones say 5–15% replies with 2–8% positive ([Pitchhired](https://pitchhired.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rates-job-search-data)). [Vendor blog. Low confidence.]
- **Best proxy: recruiter → candidate data, the reverse direction** [ATS data]:
  - Gem: a single email gets 13–15% replies, and a 4–5-step sequence gets about 2× that. The best gap between emails is about 5–6 days, and Tue/Wed mornings open best ([Gem 2022](https://www.gem.com/blog/benchmarks-and-best-practices-for-recruiting-email); [Gem 2024](https://lp.gem.com/rs/972-IVV-330/images/Guide%20to%20Email%20Outreach%20&%20Benchmarks%20Fall%202024.pdf)).
  - LinkedIn: InMails under 400 characters get 22% more responses than average, and those over 800 characters do worse ([LinkedIn Talent Blog](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/tips-for-writing-inmails-from-linkedin-recruiters)).
- **Deliverability:** Gmail requires every sender to keep spam rates below 0.3% (target below 0.1%) and to authenticate with SPF or DKIM ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). [Official]
- **Email-finder accuracy:** no independent benchmark exists. Vendor-run tests put Hunter, Apollo, RocketReach and Snov at about 75–91% accuracy, and the rankings contradict each other. Accuracy reportedly falls at companies with fewer than 20 employees. ([Anymailfinder, 2026](https://anymailfinder.com/blog/best-email-finder-tools); [b2b-email-finder, 2026](https://b2b-email-finder.com/blog/email-finder-accuracy-benchmark-2026/)) [Vendor. Treat as roughly 1 bad address in 5–10.]

## 6. Posting quality: ghost, stale, scam

- **Ghost jobs:**
  - 18–22% of Greenhouse postings in any quarter are classified as ghost jobs, method undisclosed. About 70% of Greenhouse companies posted at least one in Q2 2024. ([Greenhouse, Dec 2024](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2024-state-of-job-hunting-report); [Entrepreneur/WSJ](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/ghost-job-listings-on-the-rise-how-to-spot-avoid-experts/485494)) [ATS data]
  - Up to 21% of postings are ghost jobs, from a classifier run on Glassdoor *interview reviews* ([Ng, arXiv 2410.21771, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21771)) [Academic preprint].
- **Scams:**
  - FTC: reported job and employment-agency scam losses rose from $90M (2020) to $501M (2024) ([FTC, Mar 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87602)). Task scams were nearly 40% of 2024 job-scam reports ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/86951)). [Gov]
  - LinkedIn: 62% of professionals have seen a posting they suspected was fake. 90% of reported scam messages try to move the conversation off-platform ([LinkedIn Safety Pulse, May 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/LinkedIn-Job-Search-Safety-Pulse)) [Survey + platform data].
- **Scam signals with a source (FTC, LinkedIn):** upfront fees, early requests for sensitive information, moves to WhatsApp, Telegram or text, pay for simple "tasks" or reshipping, crypto payments.
- **Ghost-job signals:** posting age, reposting, no matching listing on the company's career site, and Glassdoor reviews saying the role was never filled. None is validated as a classifier. *Low confidence.*
- **Regulation:** since Jan 1 2026, Ontario requires employers with 25+ employees to state in postings whether a vacancy exists ([PowerPay](https://www.powerpay.ca/Resources/Ontario%E2%80%99s-New-Job-Posting-Laws-Are-Now-in-Effect-What-Employers-Need-to-Know-in-2026)).

## Implications for the skill design

1. **Optimize for interviews per approved application, not for applications sent.** Cold applications reach interview at about 3–8%. Referral and sourced routes reach it at 25–40%. Track the funnel per channel and show it to the user.
2. **Default volume:** queue **3–5 high-fit applications a day**, with a hard cap of 10, plus 1–3 outreach drafts. No study sets an optimum. This is a judgment from the volume data, the 0.4% auto-apply anecdote and Indeed's daily caps.
3. **Ranking order:**
   1. Hard requirements pass the knockout checks (authorization, location, licenses, years of experience). If any fails, drop the job.
   2. A first-degree connection works at the company. This is the strongest boost (about 7×).
   3. The job is listed on the company's own career site. Apply there, not through an aggregator.
   4. Ghost and scam risk is low.
   5. The posting is fresh. This is a weak prior.
4. **Per-job plan:**
   1. Draft a referral ask, preferring a contact in the same function.
   2. Draft a short note to the hiring manager or recruiter.
   3. Prepare the career-site application.

   The user approves each item.
5. **Tailoring:** include role-specific evidence such as projects, metrics and matching history, rather than generic polish. Prompt the user to edit every draft, since editing time predicted hires. Never use hidden text or prompt injection: it is detected and banned.
6. **Never auto-submit or automate LinkedIn or Indeed.** Both prohibit it. Hand the user links and pre-filled text.
7. **Outreach:**
   - LinkedIn notes under 400 characters, emails of about 100–150 words, one specific hook.
   - At most 2 follow-ups, 5–7 days apart, sent on a Tuesday–Thursday morning.
   - Stop on any reply.
   - Verify every email address with a second checker and show its confidence.
   - Send a few emails a day from the user's own authenticated mailbox.
8. **Filters:**
   - Reject postings with fees, moves to chat apps, crypto pay or "task" work.
   - Flag postings over 30 days old, reposted listings, jobs missing from the company's career site, and companies with ghost-job reports.
   - Read the vacancy statement where one is required.
9. **Disclosure:** don't add unprompted AI disclaimers. Answer honestly when an employer asks about AI use. The user must be able to defend every claim, since being exposed costs more trust than disclosing.
10. **Silence:** treat a cold application with no reply after about 2 weeks as dead. Ashby archives non-interviewed candidates after a median of about 6 days. Shift effort to outreach.
